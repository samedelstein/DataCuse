Syracuse's open data has a discovery problem.

Useful city data sits outside the portal. Inside it, some feeds stall and some editions lose detail.

THE RECORD

A history of what remains.

Syracuse launched its portal in 2017. Some previously listed dataset entries no longer appear in its catalog. Archived pages preserve traces, but this review did not recover a complete record of what was published and later removed, archived, or made private—or when and why.

THE UPKEEP

Some series hold. Some feeds stall.

The good: assessment final rolls cover every year from 2007–2026. Pavement ratings run continuously from 2020–2025. All five datasets declaring daily updates show October 27 edits, though one check cannot prove daily reliability.

The interruptions: Permit Requests carries a November 2025 technical-problem notice. Its latest issue date is August 16, 2025. The 2025 Part 1 crime file has a similar notice and just 33 records, dated January 1–5.

The unanswered question: the SYRCityline file is titled through January 2025 despite a monthly update declaration. Its description gives no interruption reason. Newer information may exist on SeeClickFix.

Old is not automatically neglected: 70 datasets declare no planned updates, often appropriately for historical editions. See schedules and edit dates.

THE OLD LINKS

Gone from view. Reason unclear.

Of 75 archived item links absent from today's feed, 54 return permission denied, 16 return missing or inaccessible, and five remain public. The sample includes maps and pages as well as datasets.

These links document entries missing from the current catalog. The reviewed sources do not establish whether each was deleted, archived, made private, or replaced—or when and why. An archived page is evidence of an earlier listing, not a complete removal record. Inspect all 75 checks.

THE OTHER ENTRANCES

Public, but hard to discover.

The city's ArcGIS directory lists 350 services. 218 have no matching portal dataset link. Some are copies, views, or map components; they are not 218 additional unique datasets.

Several unmatched layers return data: fire files labeled 2022–2024, housing certificates, and potholes filled in 2018. Their completeness and comparability are unverified. Search the directory.

Department pages add more: 2025 and 2026 budget workbooks, annual water reports, and Right-to-Know data ZIPs. The portal's latest titled budget edition is FY2024–25.

The October 27 searches found XLSX, PDF, and ZIP links; no standalone syr.gov-hosted CSV was confirmed. This was not a full website inventory. Workbook and ZIP contents were not parsed for that snapshot. See the source inventory.

Now easier to use: the independent DataCuse Open Data Library brings these sources together. It includes 42 downloadable tables from the budget workbooks, water report, and CRB archive, plus a searchable directory of additional ArcGIS layers. Each entry links to its source and explains its limits. Library sources were checked November 1.

THE AI TEST

Can an agent make sense of it?

There is a foundation: machine-readable catalogs, queryable APIs, and dictionary links. The city even publishes a code-violations chatbot schema.

But that schema requests owner_name, a field absent from the current layer. The catalog's contact-email field contains an unresolved template placeholder. Outage notices sit in prose, and replacement histories are hard to follow.

Better discovery, dated status notices, clear coverage periods, and versioned fields would help both residents and software. The chatbot finding is a contract mismatch—not a tested failure of a deployed bot.

Bring your own assistant: the library's AI-agent guide provides a compact catalog, field definitions, source links, and copyable prompts. Start there to find data, check its coverage, and ask questions grounded in the source.
